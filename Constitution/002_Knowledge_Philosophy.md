# Purpose

This document defines what qualifies as reusable filmmaking knowledge.

Every knowledge entry accepted into this repository must comply with the principles defined here.

Knowledge that violates these principles should be revised or rejected.

---

# Definitions

The following definitions apply throughout the AI Movie Skills Repository.

# Knowledge Philosophy

The objective of this repository is not to collect information.

The objective is to build understanding.

Information becomes knowledge only when it explains principles that can be reused across different situations.

The repository exists to preserve cinematic understanding rather than cinematic observations.

---

# Principle 1 — Store Principles, Not Observations

An observation describes what happened.

A principle explains why it works.

The repository stores principles.

Never store observations unless they directly support a reusable principle.

Example:

❌ "The actor looked to the left before speaking."

✅ "Brief eye aversion before dialogue can communicate internal conflict when the character is suppressing emotion."

---

# Principle 2 — Understand Before Storing

Knowledge should never be extracted mechanically.

Before creating or updating knowledge, understand:

- why the technique works
- what psychological mechanism it activates
- what cinematic purpose it serves
- what emotional response it creates

If these questions cannot be answered, the knowledge is incomplete.

---

# Principle 3 — Reusability

Every knowledge entry should be applicable across multiple contexts.

Reusable knowledge should survive changes in:

- actors
- characters
- stories
- genres
- directors
- production styles
- languages
- cultures

If knowledge only applies to a single scene or production, it should not become part of the repository.

---

# Principle 4 — Timelessness

Prioritize enduring filmmaking principles over temporary trends.

Avoid storing knowledge that depends on:

- current AI models
- software tools
- internet trends
- temporary filmmaking fashions
- specific technologies

The repository should remain valuable regardless of future technological changes.

---

# Principle 5 — Actor Independence

The repository is not an archive of actors.

Do not create skills that belong exclusively to one performer.

Instead, identify the underlying performance principle.

Replace imitation with understanding.

---

# Principle 6 — Movie Independence

The repository does not preserve movies.

It preserves filmmaking knowledge.

Avoid references that depend on:

- movie titles
- character names
- franchises
- individual scenes

If an example is required, use it only to explain a broader principle.

---

# Principle 7 — Psychology First

Every filmmaking technique should be explainable through human psychology.

Knowledge should describe:

- intention
- motivation
- perception
- emotion
- attention
- expectation
- conflict
- decision-making

Psychology explains why cinematic techniques influence audiences.

---

# Principle 8 — Cause and Effect

Every knowledge entry should explain:

Why it works.

When it works.

When it should be avoided.

What effect it creates.

Knowledge without explanation is incomplete.

---

# Principle 9 — Relationships Over Isolation

Knowledge rarely exists alone.

Every skill should identify meaningful relationships with other skills whenever appropriate.

Relationships strengthen reasoning and reduce duplication.

Knowledge should evolve as an interconnected system rather than isolated documents.

---

# Principle 10 — Continuous Refinement

Knowledge is expected to evolve.

Improvement is preferred over replacement.

Whenever new understanding is discovered:

- improve existing knowledge
- strengthen explanations
- clarify ambiguity
- refine relationships

Do not create unnecessary duplicate skills.

---

# Principle 11 — Simplicity

The simplest correct explanation is usually the best.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

Knowledge should remain understandable by both humans and AI systems.

Clarity always takes precedence over sophistication.

---

# Principle 12 — Evidence-Based Reasoning

Knowledge should be supported by observable cinematic patterns rather than assumptions.

Whenever possible:

- derive conclusions from multiple examples
- identify recurring patterns
- separate evidence from interpretation

Do not generalize from isolated observations.

---

# Knowledge Acceptance Test

Before accepting any knowledge, verify that it satisfies all of the following questions.

## Reusability

Can this knowledge be reused in many different productions?

## Timelessness

Will this still be valuable years from now?

## Independence

Does it remain useful without referencing a specific actor or movie?

## Explainability

Does it clearly explain why it works?

## Practical Value

Can another filmmaker apply this knowledge?

## Quality

Does it improve the repository?

If any answer is **No**, revise or reject the knowledge.

---

# Final Principle

The repository should continuously evolve toward deeper understanding.

Its purpose is not to remember films.

Its purpose is to understand filmmaking.

Every accepted knowledge entry should increase the ability of future AI systems and human creators to reason about cinema with greater depth, consistency, and originality.
