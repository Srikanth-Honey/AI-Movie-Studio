# Definition

Scene Composition is the principle of organizing visual elements within the camera frame to guide the viewer's attention, establish relationships, and convey narrative subtext.

# Purpose

This skill establishes standard guidelines for frame balance, depth, and spatial organization, ensuring visual clarity and artistic consistency across scenes.

# Psychology

Visual composition triggers instinctive human perceptual patterns. For example, positioning a character high in the frame or using low angles conveys power, while negative space or centering can convey isolation or stability.

# Story Function

Composing elements in the frame reinforces thematic progression, visual metaphors, and changes in character relationships over the course of a scene.

# Character Function

Frame placement defines a character's relative status, emotional state, and alignment with or opposition to other figures.

# Internal State

Composition reflects the emotional balance or tension within the scene's subjects.

# External Behaviour

Not applicable (this is a camera/framing design skill rather than a performer behaviour skill).

# Dialogue Guidance

Composition should support dialogue beats. For instance, shifting a character's position relative to the frame lines during a key line changes the subtextual focus.

# Voice Guidance

Not applicable.

# Body Language

Performer blocking should align with frame composition guidelines (e.g. positioning actors along the rule of thirds grid).

# Timing

Framing adjustments (like panning or zooming) should align with dramatic beats, character movements, or dialogue pauses.

# Rhythm

The editing rhythm should respect composition changes, ensuring cuts maintain visual continuity and flow between differently composed frames.

# Camera Considerations

Utilize standard framing rules (Rule of Thirds, Golden Ratio, leading lines, framing within framing) to construct the shot.

# Lighting Considerations

Lighting must support composition by highlighting the primary focal points and creating depth through shadows.

# Music & Sound Considerations

Sound design can draw attention to specific visual components established in the composition.

# Best Used When

- Designing storyboards or staging shots.
- Establishing the visual style and tone of a sequence.
- Blocking actors' movements relative to the lens.

# Avoid When

- Handheld or dynamic documentary styles where strict composition would feel artificial.
- High-intensity chaotic action where clarity is subordinate to visceral kinetics.

# Common Mistakes

- Placing key elements randomly without guiding the eye.
- Centering every subject, leading to flat and uninteresting framing.
- Failing to maintain screen direction and composition continuity across cuts.
